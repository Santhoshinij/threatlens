What ThreatLens looks for

Pressure and threats

“Act now”, “your account will be blocked”, “legal action”. Scammers rush you so you do not stop to think.

Requests for private details

OTPs, PINs, card numbers, Aadhaar, and “KYC updates”. Real banks never ask you to share these in a message.

Tricks hidden in links

Raw IP addresses, look-alike brand names, strange endings like .xyz, and shortened links that hide the real destination.

How ThreatLens decides, and where it can be wrong

Messages are scored by a text classifier trained on scam and ordinary messages. Links, including links inside messages, are scored by a second model that looks at the address itself: length, digits, subdomains, unusual endings, and look-alike brand names. The address is only read as text and is never opened.

A new scam can look harmless, and an odd but honest message can look risky. Treat the result as advice, not proof.
